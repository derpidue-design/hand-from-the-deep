# Game Design Document

## Project title

Hand from the Deep

## Genre

- sci-fi horror RPG
- narrative investigation survival game
- status-driven systemic progression
- emotionally grounded and psychologically unstable world

## Core experience

The player steps into the role of Zero2, a damaged Synthoid whose memories are incomplete and whose Zinwave node is unstable. The player explores fractured environments, makes decisions under pressure, and manages identity, emotion, continuity, and relationship bonds while confronting a reality that does not remain consistent.

## Primary themes

- identity as a living structure
- emotional stability as a survival mechanic
- memory as truth and as weapon
- replacement versus continuity
- the danger of hidden, forgotten versions of self
- the emotional cost of preserving meaning in a broken world

## Core pillars

### 1. Identity is a gameplay system

Zero2 does not just have a story; she has a condition. Her memories, node instability, and self-recognition are part of how the game works.

### 2. Reality is unstable and dangerous

The world shifts based on player state, story progress, and deeper reality events. The player cannot rely on static maps or conventional progression.

### 3. De’juir is essential

De’juir is not a generic companion. He helps stabilize Zero2 and functions as a core narrative and mechanical anchor.

### 4. Status systems drive the experience

The game should use numerous condition systems to make every encounter feel unstable and dangerous.

### 5. Horror comes from truth and self-recognition

The fear is not only enemy threat; it is the realization that identity itself may be an engineered illusion.

## Target player experience

The game should feel like:

- a layered psychological sci-fi horror narrative
- a reality-bending exploration game
- a survival-action RPG where player control has emotional cost
- a game where understanding and acceptance matter as much as combat

## Core systems

### Exploration and reality navigation

The player crosses environments that shift over time:

- black glass chambers
- maintenance corridors
- memory spaces
- the Innerspace
- corrupted regions influenced by the Deep

The game should support non-linear exploration and partial re-contextualization of spaces.

### Memory fragment retrieval

Memory fragments unlock:

- missing history
- hidden political / mechanical context
- truth about prior versions
- emotional narrative beats
- unlocking new branches or locations

### Zinwave node management

Zero2’s node is a living, unstable resource that interacts with:

- emotional load
- memory integrity
- ability usage
- enemy pressure
- the stability of reality itself

### Companion system

De’juir should influence:

- stability during corrupted events
- available support actions
- narrative trust and relationship states
- the player’s ability to survive the Deep
- endgame outcomes

### Combat and survival loop

Combat should include:

- tactical enemy pressure
- reality-disrupting hazards
- survival in unstable environments
- hostile entities generated by memory corruption or Deep contamination

Not all threats must be destroyed; some must be escaped, survived, or reinterpreted.

## Narrative structure

### Act I: Awakening

Zero2 wakes in a strange chamber. The world appears impossible. She meets De’juir. The first fractures appear.

### Act II: Fracture

Reality becomes less coherent. Zero2 learns about the Hive Cooperation, replacement models, and the possibility that she may not be unique.

### Act III: The Deep

The Innerspace and the deeper realities begin to open. The player confronts the existence of discarded versions and the Under Deep Unseen.

### Act IV: What remains

The final game state is about identity, continuity, and what is worth preserving when the world itself is not stable.

## Endgame design

The ending should be shaped by player actions, emotional state, and relationship status with De’juir. The final choice is not necessarily “win or lose”; it is whether Zero2 continues as a self, merges with continuity, or is lost to the Deep.

## Production priorities

### Minimum viable game

The first playable build should include:

- one major intro sequence
- one unstable environment
- a core status system
- a companion system with De’juir
- a major Xevil encounter
- one memory-based branch
- one playable vertical slice representing the first chapter

### Full game goal

Build a complete, playable RPG from the foundation established by the story; the aim is not only narrative adaptation, but a world that feels alive and playable.
