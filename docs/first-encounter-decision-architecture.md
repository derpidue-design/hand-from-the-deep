# First Encounter and Decision Architecture

## Overview

The first major encounter with Xevil should do more than establish a threat. It should teach the player that the game is about identity, replacement, and self-worth.

## Encounter goals

The first Xevil encounter should:

- show the world is manipulated by something intelligent and malicious
- create fear without relying on pure action pressure
- force the player to confront memory gaps and system weaknesses
- establish the idea that replacement is not an abstract concept but a core world rule

## Encounter structure

### Phase 1: Xevil appears in the corridor

The player sees a figure with a distorted body, impossible geometry, and unstable voice. The encounter feels wrong before it becomes violent.

### Phase 2: status pressure

Xevil triggers:

- Emotional Flux rises
- Memory Integrity falls
- Continuity Fracture begins to build
- the environment warps and begins to behave unpredictably

### Phase 3: identity challenge

Xevil speaks about the possibility of other versions, replacement, and the concept of Zero3. This is the moment where identity becomes the core threat rather than a physical enemy.

### Phase 4: critical response

The player is forced to choose how to respond. The encounter should include a direct emotional decision, not just a combat win condition.

Possible player choices:
- resist Xevil and hold on to the self
- allow the fracture to widen and risk losing continuity
- lean on De’juir for stabilization
- pursue memory fragments or clues to better understand the threat

## Decision architecture

The game should track narrative decisions through a small but meaningful matrix:

- trust De’juir or question him
- resist the idea of replacement or begin to believe Xevil’s logic
- pursue emotional certainty or system certainty
- accept help or reject it in favor of a more isolated identity path

These decisions should affect:
- later narrative paths
- relationship state with De’juir
- future region access
- how later key scenes unfold

## Encounter importance

This encounter is essential because it teaches the player that the game is not about traditional enemy combat. The real enemy is the idea that Zero2 can be overwritten, replaced, or erased.

## Resulting effect on progression

After the encounter:

- the state of Zero2’s Continuity Fracture should increase or decrease based on player response
- De’juir synchronization may rise if the player chooses stability
- memory fragments become more important in the next region
- the world opens into a more dangerous space, creating a natural transition

## Production requirement

This encounter should be designed to be highly replayable in terms of interpretation and consequence, while still being recognizable as the same story beat.
