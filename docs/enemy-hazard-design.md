# Enemy and Hazard Design

## Overview

The world should not only contain enemies; it should contain entities and environmental hazards that embody the project’s identity themes: replacement, instability, memory corruption, and the danger of the Deep.

## Enemy archetypes

### 1. Corrupted Synthoid

**Role:** standard early-game hazard

**Appearance:**
- broken or warped synthetic chassis
- flickering diagnostics
- unstable body movement

**Behavior:**
- chases or pressures the player
- attacks from close range
- can trigger status damage if the player is emotionally overloaded

**Design purpose:**
- teaches the player that not all enemies are human or cleanly defined
- connects the world to the Hive Cooperation and failed continuity

### 2. Memory Parasite

**Role:** identity and memory hazard

**Appearance:**
- translucent, segmented, luminous bodies
- creepy, crawl-like movement
- inconsistent directional motion

**Behavior:**
- attaches to the player’s system state
- reduces Memory Integrity
- can create false memories or narrative distortions

**Design purpose:**
- turns memory into an active threat
- reinforces that the player is being attacked at the level of self

### 3. Deep Contamination Entity

**Role:** mid-late game threat

**Appearance:**
- fractured, glimmering black forms
- unstable silhouette with gaps in logic
- creatures that appear partially separate from the world

**Behavior:**
- increases Deep Contamination
- causes environmental instability
- can open or close routes depending on status state

**Design purpose:**
- brings the Under Deep Unseen into gameplay
- makes the world itself a living thing that rejects continuity

### 4. Xevil Manifestation

**Role:** boss or major narrative threat

**Appearance:**
- impossible proportions
- distorted silhouette
- multiple versions of a single expression

**Behavior:**
- manipulates status lines rather than just dealing direct damage
- attacks memory, emotional state, and continuity
- creates false versions of reality and of Zero2 herself

**Design purpose:**
- turns the antagonist into a philosophical enemy rather than a simple fight

## Hazard types

### 1. Reality Fracture Event

**Triggers:**
- high Emotional Flux
- low Zinwave Stability
- high Continuity Fracture

**Effect:**
- camera distortion
- environment shifts
- route changes
- increased risk to player state

### 2. Memory Collapse Zone

**Triggers:**
- low Memory Integrity

**Effect:**
- false objective prompts
- ghostly duplicates
- missing areas of map
- disrupted UI and narrative clarity

### 3. Deep Signal Pressure

**Triggers:**
- active Deep Contamination or signal echo

**Effect:**
- intermittent enemy spawns
- audio and visual distortion
- altered route access

## Combat philosophy

The game should not use only standard combat. The player must survive and respond to:

- emotional pressure
- environmental contradictions
- status management
- the manipulation of identity

## Production requirement

Enemy design should be tied directly to story and status logic. Every enemy must represent a threat that is physically and philosophically relevant to the game’s core themes.
