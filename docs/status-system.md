# Status System Specification

This document defines the core status systems that drive the gameplay in Hand from the Deep.

## Design goal

The status system should reflect identity, instability, emotional strain, memory fracture, and continuity collapse. These systems should be major gameplay levers, not just UI decoration.

## Required statuses

### 1. Zinwave Stability

Measures how strongly Zero2 remains anchored to her self and continuity.

Low stability leads to:
- fractured perception
- memory gaps
- reduced ability accuracy
- altered enemy perception
- deeper reality distortion

High stability allows:
- clearer memory recall
- better interaction with the world
- stronger resistance to Deep corruption
- improved use of reality-sensitive actions

### 2. Emotional Flux

Tracks how much emotional pressure is destabilizing Zero2’s internal systems.

It should respond to:
- fear
- grief
- anger
- confusion
- attachment
- confrontation with the truth

This is not a generic meter; it should be layered and tied to narrative events.

### 3. Memory Integrity

Determines how much of Zero2’s past remains coherent and useful.

Low memory integrity brings:
- missing objectives
- misread events
- false assumptions
- identity confusion
- broken environmental reading

High memory integrity improves:
- puzzle interpretation
- enemy reading
- truth discovery
- relationship clarity

### 4. Continuity Fracture

Represents the gap between the version of Zero2 that exists now and the identity she may have lost.

This state can trigger:
- self-contradiction in dialogue and narrative
- misalignment of world events
- temporary identity disorientation
- state-specific branching outcomes

### 5. Deep Contamination

A condition caused by the Under Deep Unseen and Xevil’s influence.

Symptoms include:
- warped environment behavior
- corruption of interactable spaces
- stronger and stranger enemies
- degraded reality logic

This status should escalate as the player enters deeper, more dangerous spaces.

### 6. Synchronization

Tracks the relationship and system alignment between Zero2 and De’juir.

High synchronization enables:
- combined abilities
- stronger stability during fractured events
- improved chances of survival in deep spaces

Low synchronization creates:
- instability
- weakened support actions
- heavier risk during high-pressure scenes

### 7. Node Overload

An acute condition where Zero2’s node can no longer safely process emotional or environmental stress.

This should feel like a critical escalation state; it is not just damage.

### 8. Residual Signal / Signal Echo

The lingering effect of the signal that survived the confrontation with Xevil.

It may trigger:
- false memories
- whispers or distorted environmental hints
- contact from deeper reality
- alternate narrative routes

### 9. Obsolescence Pressure

This is the feeling of being replaceable, outdated, or disposable.

This mechanic should feed directly into identity conflict and emotional vulnerability.

### 10. Anti-Drift Anchoring

Represents the stabilizing force created through De’juir’s continuity integration.

This is a key late-game system tied to the I.N.T. Engine and Zero2’s growing stability.

## Interaction rules

Status systems should influence each other:

- Emotional Flux increases Zinwave instability
- Zinwave instability worsens Memory Integrity
- Deep Contamination amplifies all other status risks
- Synchronization counters Continuity Fracture
- Node Overload can force narrative branch states
- Obsolescence Pressure makes the player more vulnerable to replacement logic and false identities

## Design requirement

These statuses must be readable to players, meaningful to gameplay, and tightly aligned to the story’s central themes.

## Example usage

If Zero2 enters a deep spatial fracture while emotionally overloaded:

- Zinwave Stability drops
- Memory Integrity suffers
- Continuity Fracture rises
- Deep Contamination may begin to build
- De’juir synchronization becomes more important

This creates a layered survival problem rather than a simple health bar problem.
